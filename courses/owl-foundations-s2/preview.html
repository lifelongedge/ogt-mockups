1.1

Two totems, one habit

reflect

Skill Name the Tortoise and the Owl, and say what each one asks of you at the screen

Terms tortoise owl

Two totems, one habit

A totem animal is an animal a people, a clan or a person takes as an emblem and as a standard of conduct: not a mascot, but a reminder of how to behave. The word comes from the Ojibwe doodem, a clan's kin-mark, and reached English through an eighteenth-century trader's account of the Great Lakes nations. Many traditions have something like it; the idea is that you keep an animal in mind so that its way of being becomes yours when it matters.

Trading is an inner game. The chart is the same for everyone; what differs is the state of the person looking at it, and that state is what a totem is for. The Owl Group keeps two: the Tortoise and the Owl.

The Tortoise is the long horizon and the shell. It compounds slowly, takes shelter in bear markets, and does not follow the crowd into exuberance. When the Tortoise is on the screen, the question is "am I still in the game next year?"

The Owl is the rational sniper. Quiet observation, self-knowledge, patience without a deadline, awareness without judgement. When the Owl is on the screen, the question is "what is actually in front of me, and is it one of the states I have rehearsed?"

The Tortoise Trading Community of Practice was founded in 1996 by Dr. Ken Long and Robert Gardner as a voluntary network of private and professional traders. The Owl Group is where its method is taught, and these two totem animals are its own pairing; other traditions keep other animals.

The habit the two share is what this course drills: separating the process from the outcome. A trade framing is right or wrong on the bars that were visible at the time. Whether the trade paid is a different fact, logged separately.

Before you continue. Open any symbol in the Viewer, drag the as-of slider back a year, and step forward one week at a time. Say aloud, at each step, which totem the chart is asking for. There is nothing to mark here; the point is to hear yourself say it.

2.3

Regression lines: RL10, RL30, RL90, RL270

read

Skill Name four regression lines as four horizons, say whether they stack, and know what a crossover is

The method fits a least-squares line to the last 10, 30, 90 and 270 closes and plots each at its end point. RL10 is the trader's price, RL30 trend health, RL90 the institutional trend, RL270 long-term fair value. When price and the four lines sit in one order the horizons agree; when they interleave they do not. When a faster regression line crosses a slower one, the method calls it a regression line crossover, RLCO. The classic form is RL10 crossing RL30: the trader's price changing sides against trend health. RLCO is the method's first-generation recognition; the crossing you meet in the next lesson, RL10 crossing the Dragon, is its second generation, and the Owl setup in unit 4 is built on that.

3.1

The risk box: the Frog as 1R

measure

Skill Measure any move in a risk box built from the symbol's own daily ranges

A risk box is a volatility unit sized from the symbol's own daily ranges, and the method has more than one. The Frog is the standard deviation of the daily range over the 30 sessions strictly before today. RangeStat is the mean range plus one standard deviation over the same 30 sessions. Average True Range is a third. Foundations uses the Frog as 1R; the choice of box is the trader's, the discipline of sizing in a box rather than in dollars is the method's. The Frog lines on the chart sit one to four Frogs above and below today's open.

3.2

xR is not 1R: MMRB and R_exec

measure

Skill Place an execution stop at a structure and say the distance in risk boxes

Sizing risk and execution risk are two different numbers, and this is where they part company. 1R is the risk box from the last lesson: the symbol's own daily movement, the unit you size the position in. xR is the distance from your entry to the stop you would actually place, at a structure the chart gives you. The method carries two execution units on the daily chart. The minimum manageable risk box, MMRB, is the smallest structural box a local swing pivot or an RL10 trough allows, and the method calibrates it to 0.2R to 0.33R. R_exec is the setup-day range: yesterday's range plus a nickel at each end, which is exactly the stop distance the frame in the next lesson uses. Example, on IWM at the close of 2025-08-13. The level is the price you drag onto the chart to be your stop, and entry is the as-of close, 228.25, the only entry that bar shows you. The nearest pivot below is yesterday's low, 218.31. That distance is 9.94 points, about 8.0 risk boxes. Against an MMRB of 0.2R to 0.33R the nearest daily pivot sits more than twenty times further out than the box the method aims for. The same bar's R_exec is yesterday's range plus the two nickels, 5.85 points or about 4.7 risk boxes: tighter, and still nothing like 0.33R. That gap is the lesson. Sizing in the wide unit while stopping at the tight one is what makes a stopped trade cost a fraction of a unit, and the daily chart shows you how far a daily structure sits from that tight unit: the method reaches the MMRB on the execution timeframe, not here. Now the same on your own symbol, and try a second level closer to the close to watch xR fall.

3.3

The trade frame, both directions

frame

Skill Frame a trade both ways from the same bars and let the gate decide

A frame answers six questions before any order: where do I get in, where am I wrong, where is the payoff, how far is the stop in risk units, does the payoff beat the stop by the method's gate, and what would reverse the whole idea. The first five come off the chart; the sixth is yours. Long: entry a nickel above yesterday's high, initial stop a nickel below yesterday's low, target the 10-day high, over the 10 sessions ending at the as-of bar. Short is the mirror: entry a nickel below yesterday's low, stop a nickel above yesterday's high, target the 10-day low. The stop distance is yesterday's range plus the two nickels; the method calls it one unit of range execution risk, R_exec. Reward-to-risk is target minus entry, divided by entry minus stop, and the method rejects any frame under 2.0. Example, on IWM at the close of 2025-08-12: yesterday's high 218.83 and low 216.98 give a long frame with entry 218.88, stop 216.93 and target 224.06. Risk is 1.95 points, reward 5.18, ratio 2.66: the long passes. The short frame is entry 216.93, stop 218.88, target 209.61: reward 7.32 over risk 1.95 is 3.76, so the short passes too. Both pass on that bar; the frame has no opinion about direction, only about geometry. Step one bar forward and neither passes. Now the same on your own symbol.

Unit 5The operating cycle and the inner game

5.1

Plan, Prepare, Execute, Assess on one chart

walkthrough

Skill Walk the master cycle end to end on a chart you have already framed

Terms owl tortoise

Plan, Prepare, Execute, Assess on one chart

The method's master cycle has four steps, and every lesson in this course belongs to one of them. Walk it once, end to end, on a chart you framed in unit 4.

Plan. Top-down: index first (lesson 1.2), then the symbol's season (2.4), then the scan for a critical state (1.3, 4.2). The output of Plan is a short list of symbols worth framing, not a trade.

Prepare. Frame the trade both ways (3.3). Six questions in each direction: where is entry, where is the initial stop, where is the target, what is xR in risk boxes (3.2), what does the 2.0 gate say, and what reverses the framing. The output of Prepare is a frame that can be falsified by the next bar.

Execute. The D3A loop (4.4): Detect the state on the as-of bar, Decide, Deliver the order, Assess the fill. Battle drills (3.4) fire at known R levels with no thinking. The TWPM gate (5.2) is checked before Deliver, every time.

Assess. The after-action review with FFTC (5.3), the same day. Facts, Feelings, Thoughts, Conclusions. The conclusion feeds tomorrow's Plan, which is what makes the cycle a loop and not a list.

Do it now. Open any symbol you have already framed (the examples in this course use IWM in August 2025), park the as-of slider where you framed it, and say each step aloud as you move through the lessons named above. Mark this lesson done when you have spoken all four.

5.4

Zero State and Operation FIG

reflect

Skill Arrive at the screen centred, and leave the last trade behind you

Terms owl

Zero State and Operation FIG

Zero State is the baseline you trade from: unhurried, cleared of anxiety and of magical thinking. The Owl is the reminder. The method holds that execution is decoupled from personal profit and loss, and Zero State is what makes that possible in a body wired for fight or flight.

Operation FIG is the daily boundary routine that gets you there. Three lines, spoken or written, before the first chart opens.

Forgiveness. Release yesterday's losses and mistakes. They were data, and the data has been logged.

Intention. Set today's execution identity in one sentence. "I am the operator who follows the frame."

Gratitude. Frame market feedback as information you were given, not a verdict on you.

The Owl meditation

A structured visualisation the Owl Group uses to arrive at Zero State. Read it slowly, or record it in your own voice. Two to five minutes. The app plays no audio and keeps no timer.

1. Sit where you trade. Feet flat, hands still. Breathe out longer than you breathe in, three times. 2. Picture the Owl on a branch above a clearing at dusk. It is not hunting yet. It is watching. 3. Notice what the Owl is not doing. It is not hurrying. It is not hoping. It is not replaying the last hunt. 4. Bring one thing from yesterday's session to the clearing and set it down. A loss, a missed frame, a rule broken. Look at it as data. Leave it there. 5. Say today's intention once, as the Owl would hear it: short, in the present tense, about execution and not about outcome. 6. Let the clearing go dark. Open your eyes to the screen with the Owl's eyes: quiet, patient, non-judging.

Return to step 1 at any point in the session when you notice the Monkey mind reacting to a bar.

This lesson has no chart and no check, and the app does not invent one. Mark it done when you have run FIG once, today.

ReferenceOwl readings

A method owns the reading, never the definition. The rule and formula for each term come from the platform vocabulary; the reading below is the Owl Group's.
tortoise
The long-perspective trader who takes shelter in bear markets and stays out of the crowd's exuberance. Survive, then compound.taught at 1.1
owl
The rational sniper: quiet observation, self-knowledge, patience without a deadline, awareness without judgement. The stance the method asks for at the screen.taught at 1.1
critical_state
A moment where an outsized directional move has room and the cost of finding out is small. Where the method looks first; not a call. Distinct from edge-vocab's ccs, the Compound Critical State.taught at 1.3
rl
Four regression lines, four horizons on one chart. RL10 is the trader's price, RL30 trend health, RL90 the institutional trend, RL270 long-term fair value. Their order against price is the stack.taught at 2.3
rlco
The family name for a faster regression line crossing a slower one. RL10 crossing RL30 is the classic; RL10 crossing the Dragon is the second generation; the SSC and the Owl setup are recognised from it.taught at 2.3
zbands
The River at one sigma, the Floodplain at two, the Z3 redline at three. About two closes in three live in the River; a close in the Floodplain is stretch or abnormal momentum.taught at 2.1
dragon
The noise boundary. Inside the skin and belly, movement is noise; RL10 leaving the band is the event every crossing pattern is built on.taught at 2.2
seasons
The regime read that comes before the setup read. Spring and Summer are rising, Fall and Winter falling; the same structure is read differently in each.taught at 2.4
macdrl
The oscillator the seasons are read from: the 10-bar mean of RL10 minus its 30-bar mean, with its sign and slope giving the four seasons.taught at 2.4
frog
One of the method's risk boxes: the symbol's own volatility unit from the 30 prior daily ranges. Foundations measures every distance in Frogs.taught at 3.1
r10
RangeStat is a second risk box, mean range plus one standard deviation over the same 30 sessions; R10 is a tenth of it, the spacing of the platform's levels and ladders.taught at 3.1
1r
One risk box, the Frog in this course. The sizing unit, distinct from xR, the execution stop distance at a pivot. The method sizes in 1R and stops at xR.taught at 3.1
frame_the_trade
A falsifiable structure with no opinion: entry past yesterday's extreme, initial stop past the other, the 10-day extreme as the target, and a 2.0 reward-to-risk gate. Built both ways from the same bars.taught at 3.3
battle_drill
A rehearsed, zero-thinking response at a known R level. No Lose plus Dinner for Two ratchets the stop to +0.5R once the trade has paid 0.7R; the 2R drill locks or trails.taught at 3.4
yz
Yesterday's high, low and close. Inside is chop; a close past either edge is new conviction. The smallest level the method uses.taught at 4.1
pdh
Yesterday's high, the upper edge of the Yellow Zone and the level a long frame's entry is placed a nickel above.taught at 4.1
pinch
Compression: the Z3 envelope squeezed to a small number of Frogs. A critical state to notice, never a call.taught at 4.2
psar
A mechanical trailing stop on price or on RL10. The flip is the moment the stop is hit and re-seeded on the other side; on RL10 it is the fifth point of the Owl setup.taught at 4.3
ssc
The Supported Spring Crossing, the first-generation recognition the Owl setup grew from: a spring off support with RL10 crossing the Dragon and RL30 turning.taught at 4.3
