Course card heroes: six options

The cards on /courses and the store open with a 7-second loop. Today that loop is four AI stock stills (a ship's bell, an hourglass, brass gears, a chimp) with a caption. Nothing in it comes from the course, and nothing in it tells a trader why the course is worth $49. Every option below is built only from what is inside the course: its chapter list, its flashcards, Dr. Long's own charts and his own words. All eighteen heroes run on one clock; scrub it to see any beat.

What the cards show today

Four generated stills per course with a caption; the course title repeated in the corner.

What a trader readsA stock-photo mood board. Bell, meadow, hourglass: "critical state" is a caption on a picture that could sell a watch. The title is already printed under the card, so the hero spends its top-right corner repeating it.
What it costsFour image-model calls per course (the Imagen endpoint died 2026-11-17 and the script fell back to Gemini), a Ken Burns pass, a 3–7 MB GIF fallback. None of it is reusable for the course itself.
VERDICT

What I would build

Option 7, which is option 1 carrying option 3's chapter strip, for the three chart courses; option 1's drawn picture (the teeter-totter, the four intelligences) for the two Mental Model courses. Reasons:

  1. It is the one thing the current heroes cannot be accused of: it shows the course. The chart is a frame from the course's own walk-through; the words are his; the chapters are the real ones.
  2. The pieces already exist. The stills are in courses/<course>/stills/, the chapter times are in each scenes.json, the overlay is the Desk style the animation kit already draws, so the hero is a 7-second render of the same kit, with no image-model call.
  3. It positions the buyer as a trader, not a reader: a chart with a frame on it says "you will learn to see this", a hourglass says "please feel something about time".

Options 4 and 5 are the strongest for the sales page itself, where there is room and the visitor is already interested: the flashcard proves there is substance to drill, the competency list answers "what will I be able to do". They are weaker as a card hero because a card has three seconds of attention and both need reading.

Facts only: every claim in every hero is a chapter title, a flashcard, or a scene from the published cut; option 5's lines are the skills the chapters teach, never an outcome. Frame positions on the charts in this mockup are illustrative; the production render places them from the walk-through's own scene.
