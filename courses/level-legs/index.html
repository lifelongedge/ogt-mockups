Between Two Levels

Course concept, mockup with live data. The same two levels, say the open to +1 Frog, or −7 R10 to −8 R10, are joined by very different paths. The measures we already draw (RL10, the dragon, the mean, z1, z2) name each path. Every leg below is a real one, from our own 1-minute bars.

At the fork: x+1 or x−1?

Price has just touched level x. Which does it reach first, the next level on (continue) or the one it came from (revert)? Measured on 20 liquid names, every touch since 2022: the first half of the years to look, the second half (from July 2024) opened once to check. Decide at the close of the touch bar, fill at the next bar's open, stop at one rung, target the other.

The readings move the odds, and the price with them

Each dot is one reading at the touch, e.g. "arrived as a Skin ride", "z of 3 or more", "RL10 already pointing back". Up is how often price continued; across is where the fill sat between the two rungs. On the line, the odds are exactly what the distances to the two rungs pay for: betting either side earns nothing. Dots off the line would be an edge.

Waiting does not help

Read again 1, 2, 3, 5 and 10 bars after the touch. The odds get easier to call as time passes (the separation score rises), but only because price has already moved toward one rung, so the payoff shrinks to match. Adding the shape, the dragon, RL10 and z to "where is price now" adds nothing, and neither does combining them: a model that can weigh every combination of readings (shape with spine with RL10 with z …) scores within 0.005 R of position alone. Second-half years, model fitted on the first half.

A plan with a falsifier

The paths do not call the side, but they can test it. At the touch, state the side, the two rungs, and the paths that must not happen if you are right. When one appears, the call is wrong: close at the next bar's open instead of waiting for the stop. Measured on the same touches, every rule on its own, first half and second half. Combining paths (close at the first of two, or only once both have appeared) lands on the same trade-offs as the single rules: the spine and RL10 rules fire on mostly the same trades.

The call

What the course would teach

  1. The rulers. R10, R5 and Frog from the last 30 sessions, laid off from the open. Why each ruler gets its own bar size: R10 on 1 and 3 minutes, R5 on 3 and 10, Frog on 10 and 30.
  2. A leg. The last touch of one level to the first touch of the next. Inside a leg price never goes back to where it started, so the whole picture lives between two lines.
  3. Reading the path. Where the closes sit against the dragon spine and skin, which way RL10 points, how often the mean is crossed, how far the z bands are stretched.
  4. The seven shapes. One chapter each, with today's legs beside the old ones. A shape names the path that was taken.
  5. The fork. At a level, continue or revert is close to even (49 to 51 % on every ruler). What the shape and the indicators change is where price sits between the two rungs, and the distances change the payoff by the same amount. The break-even line, drawn on the trader's own chart.
  6. A plan with a falsifier. State the side, the two rungs and the paths that must not happen. Closing on a forbidden path leaves the average result where it was; it halves what a wrong call costs and how long it is held, and gives up a fifth to two fifths of the calls that would have reached the next level (Frog 10m to R10 1m). The trade-off against a tighter stop, on the trader's own symbols.
  7. Your own tape. The same measures on the trader's own symbols in Edge S2, so a shape is named while it forms and the two rungs either side are on the chart before the touch.
