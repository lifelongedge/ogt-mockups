The Dots Are One Bank

The PSAR dots are one edge of a channel. When the dots sit under price (the flip is long), the far edge is the highs: the high of the leg and the turn of the RL10. The wind is the day's direction: easterlies blow the boat up the chart, westerlies blow it down. The side you sail decides whether the day is a run or a beat. With the wind, the legs are long and the dots fall far astern. Against it, the boat still crosses to touch the other bank, then gives it back on every tack. A real SPY session is drawn below, twice.

Two winds, and the rare clean run

Click a card to load that session above. The drawing on each card is the idea; the numbers under it are measured on the session named. "With the wind" means the legs on the day's side (long in an easterly, short in a westerly); points per bar is the close at the end of a leg less the close at its start, over the bars in the leg.

Easterlies · a bullish wind: long is the run

The wind blows the boat up the chart. Sailing long is running with it: the up-legs are long, the dots step up under the boat, the far bank is the highs. Sailing short is beating into it: short legs that reach the dots above and give the ground straight back.

Westerlies · a bearish wind: short is the run

The wind blows the boat down the chart. Sailing short is the run: the down-legs are long, the dots sit above, the far bank is the lows. Trying to go long is the beat: the boat does get up to touch the other side, and loses ground on every tack. The belly of the RL10 is where the PSAR flips, and that is where the short re-enters.

The clean run · rare, in either wind

Most days, even with the wind, are sailed in tacks. Once in a while one leg carries nearly the whole day: the boat never needs the far bank and the dots are left far astern. That is the day not to take the first wiggle. First wiggle in a major trend: trust the PSAR.

What this page measures

PSAR: step 0.02, max 0.20, on the price source chosen above (RL10 feeds high = low = RL10; null RL10 falls back to the close). A leg is a run of bars with one PSAR side; a tack is a flip. A leg's points are its last close less its first close; points per bar divides by the bars in the leg; the side numbers sum the legs on each side. The wind on a session nobody picked is a rule, not a judgement: net ÷ range above +0.25 is easterlies, below −0.25 westerlies, between is light air; a day whose longest leg runs 30+ bars with net ÷ range beyond ±0.6 is also marked a clean run. The three presets name their wind outright.

Decisions for the owner

  1. The far bank. The leg's highs, the RL10 turn (marked ▽), or both. The page draws both so you can see where they differ.
  2. PSAR source. Ken's setting is the RL10. Default here is RL10; the bars version is one click away.
  3. The three sessions. Presets are SPY 2026-10-21 (easterlies), 2026-10-16 (westerlies), 2026-11-05 (a westerly clean run). Any of the 30 SPY or QQQ sessions can replace them from the picker.
  4. The efficiency number. Points per bar by side is on the cards; the share of the day's range each side carried is the other candidate.
  5. The wind words. Easterlies / westerlies with the bullish / bearish gloss, or plain sailing words (running, beating).
  6. Finish style. Ink Board, Field Notes, Desk or the new stage rules from the Stanford study; the river panel is the one new scene kind the kit does not have yet.
