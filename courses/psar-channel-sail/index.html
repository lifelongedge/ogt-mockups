The Dots Are One Bank

The PSAR dots are one edge of a channel. When the dots sit under price (the flip is long), the far edge is the highs: the high of the leg and the turn of the RL10. Price gets up the channel the way a sailboat gets up a river against the wind: by tacking from bank to bank. A real SPY session is drawn below, twice.

Three winds, three kinds of day

Click a card to load that session above. The drawing on each card is the idea; the numbers under it are measured on the session named.

Easterlies · the boat makes way, by zigzags

The day is with you, but not straight. Each leg runs from the dots to the far bank and comes about. The dots step up behind the boat; they are the trailing stop while the RL10 slope is up.

Westerlies · losing ground, still touching the far bank

The day is against you. The boat still crosses to touch the other side on every counter-leg, and that touch at the dots is where the short re-enters: the belly of the RL10 is where the PSAR flips.

Downwind run · the rare straight leg

Only now and then does the boat get to run. One leg carries most of the day, price rides the far bank, the dots fall far behind. This is the day not to take the first wiggle.

How it would be said

  1. Every PSAR dot is a bank. When the dots are under the price, the near bank is under you. The far bank is the highs: the high of this leg, and the turn of the RL10.drawn: the chart, dots only, then the far bank fades in on the highs
  2. A sailboat cannot go straight up a river into the wind. It tacks: out to one bank, come about, out to the other. Price does the same between the dots and the highs.drawn: the river panel, the boat's first two legs

  4. Easterlies: the wind lets you make way, by zigzags. As long as the RL10 slope is up I'm using the PSAR as my trailing stop.Ken, 5DD mini-lessons transcript · card 1

What this page measures

PSAR: step 0.02, max 0.20, on the price source chosen above (RL10 feeds high = low = RL10; null RL10 falls back to the close). A leg is a run of bars with one PSAR side; a tack is a flip. A leg reached the far bank when a long leg's high got to the previous long leg's high (or a short leg's low to the previous short leg's low). The wind read on a session nobody picked is a rule, not a judgement: net ÷ range above +0.6 with a leg of 30+ bars is a run; above +0.25 is easterlies; below −0.5 is westerlies; anything else is light air.

Decisions for the owner

  1. The far bank. The leg's highs (drawn), the RL10 turn (marked ▽), or both. The page draws both so you can see where they differ.
  2. PSAR source. Ken's setting is the RL10. Default here is RL10; the bars version is one click away.
  3. The three sessions. Presets are SPY 2026-10-21 (easterlies), 2026-11-20 (westerlies), 2026-11-09 (run). Any of the 30 SPY or QQQ sessions can replace them from the picker.
  4. The wind words. Your easterlies / westerlies / downwind run, or plain sailing words (headwind, crosswind, running). The compass copy follows whichever you pick.
  5. Finish style. Ink Board, Field Notes, Desk or the new stage rules from the Stanford study; the river panel is the one new scene kind the kit does not have yet.
