Pricing direction

Owner, 2026-11-18

Maximize total revenue, choosing the options that maximize future sales. The same $1,000 can come three ways:

1 × $1,000a) one customer paying $1,000
10 × $100b) ten customers paying $100
100 × $10c) a hundred customers paying $10

For the long term, c is the better option because it touches far more people. Each of the hundred is someone who has seen the work, can buy the next course, can move toward coaching, and can tell another trader about it.

The list (773 active subscribers) is too small for fine-grained price tests, so the price is set by this principle rather than by an A/B/C split.

A first-time buyer judging quality

Someone who has never bought from us cannot tell what a course is worth before paying for it. Rather than a first-buyer discount, which a second email address gets around, parts of each course are free to try on request: the person asks, and we send them a sample (for example the first chapter) to watch.

Asking twice from another address gets the same free sample again, so there is nothing to abuse. Each request also tells us who is interested in which course, which a discount code does not. Panel 1b shows the request and what gets sent.

On list price, a sale cuts what the student pays and leaves the credit alone; the $1,000 cap still holds coaching at $1,997. On price paid, a 50% sale halves the credit, and the cap takes four courses to reach instead of two.

8 · Open decisions
  1. Which variant. A gives a reason to buy a second course (it doubles the credit) and stops at a floor of $1,997 for coaching. B is the simplest to explain. C adds a cheaper first step but needs a new $25 product.
  2. The cap. A shows $1,000. Without one, five courses would take coaching down to about $500.
  3. Past buyers. About 18 people bought courses, some at $19.97 or $29.97. Count their purchases (10× what they paid), or start the credit from launch day.
  4. Observer tier ($997 a year). Credit on Active only, or also on Observer with a lower cap (for example $300).
  5. Credit during a sale. Recommended: on list price, so "50% off" and "+ $500 toward coaching" never contradict each other. Counting on price paid makes every sale halve the credit, which the sales page then has to explain.
  6. How a sale runs. One entry per sale (name, percent, start and end dates in ET). During that window the engine adds a Stripe coupon to every course checkout (no new prices), the store and sales pages show the struck-through price and the end date, and the email links show the sale price. Outside the window everything reverts on its own. Coaching itself is never on sale, so the credit and a sale do not stack on coaching.
  7. Rules that hold in every variant: only paid purchases earn credit (gifts such as Part 2 for past buyers earn nothing), a refund removes its credit, one year to use it, applied automatically at the coaching checkout by the buyer's email.
